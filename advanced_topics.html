Advanced Topics in Economics

This curriculum branches out from the Core Undergraduate Curriculum into five distinct specialization tracks. Learners should select one or two tracks aligned with their intellectual goals or career path rather than attempting to complete every track sequentially.

  • Microeconomic Theory & Industrial Organization — Rigorous formal analysis of individual decision-making, strategic game theory, antitrust economics, and public policy design.
  • Macroeconomics & Monetary Systems — Advanced study of business cycle fluctuations, dynamic growth theory, banking mechanisms, and central bank policy.
  • Econometrics & Causal Inference — The empirical toolkit used by modern economists to identify causal effects, design quasi-experiments, and analyze observational data.
  • Development Economics & Globalization — Applied investigation into the microeconomics of poverty, randomized policy evaluations, international trade models, and institutional divergence.
  • Behavioral & Experimental Economics — Integration of psychological realism into formal economic models, examining decision-making biases, behavioral finance, and choice architecture.

Microeconomic Theory & Industrial Organization

This track deepens core microeconomics through formal general equilibrium analysis, strategic game theory, market power dynamics, and the economic theory of public policy.

Intermediate Microeconomic Analysis: 14.04 Intermediate Microeconomic Theory (MIT OpenCourseWare) - A complete university course with video lectures by Prof. Robert Townsend, formalizing consumer choice, general equilibrium, and mechanism design.

Game Theory & Strategic Thinking: ECON 159: Game Theory (Yale Open Courses) - A comprehensive recorded lecture series by Prof. Ben Polak covering backward induction, subgame perfection, repeated games, signaling, and auction theory.

Industrial Organization & Market Power: 14.271 Industrial Organization I (MIT OpenCourseWare) - An advanced course with video lectures by Prof. Glenn Ellison integrating formal oligopoly models, price discrimination, dynamic competition, and empirical market structure analysis.

Public Finance & Government Policy: 14.41 Public Finance and Public Policy (MIT OpenCourseWare) - A complete course with video lectures by Prof. Jonathan Gruber analyzing tax incidence, social insurance programs, public goods, and externalities.

Macroeconomics & Monetary Systems

This track advances macroeconomic analysis into modern growth models, business cycle fluctuations, central banking operations, and the balance-sheet dynamics of global monetary systems.

Intermediate Macroeconomic Theory: 14.05 Intermediate Macroeconomics (MIT OpenCourseWare) - Provides rigorous lecture notes and problem sets covering consumption smoothing, investment dynamics, real business cycles, and fiscal policy analysis.

Monetary Economics & Central Banking: Economics of Money and Banking (Columbia University on Coursera) - A free-to-audit course taught by Prof. Perry Mehrling that reconstructs modern macroeconomics around balance sheets, dealer markets, liquidity, and central bank operations.

Advanced Macroeconomic Modeling: Advanced Macroeconomics (David Romer) - The definitive upper-level textbook presenting the formal mathematics and empirical evidence behind dynamic stochastic general equilibrium, New Keynesian price stickiness, and unemployment models.

Theory of Modern Economic Growth: Introduction to Modern Economic Growth (Daron Acemoglu) - An authoritative treatise synthesizing neoclassical growth models, endogenous technological innovation, human capital accumulation, and institutional divergence.

Econometrics & Causal Inference

This track provides the methodological toolkit required to evaluate economic policies and estimate causal relationships using observational and quasi-experimental data.

Applied Microeconometrics: Mostly Harmless Econometrics: An Empiricist's Companion (Joshua D. Angrist and Jörn-Steffen Pischke) - The foundational practitioner's guide to the credibility revolution, detailing how applied researchers implement regression, instrumental variables, differences-in-differences, and regression discontinuity designs.

Research Design & Causal Identification: The Effect: An Introduction to Research Design and Causality (Nick Huntington-Klein) - A free, complete online textbook covering directed acyclic graphs (DAGs), potential outcomes, and identification strategies for isolating causal effects from observational data.

Computational Econometrics with R: Introduction to Econometrics with R (Christoph Hanck, Martin Arnold, Alexander Gerber, and Martin Schmelzer) - A free, interactive open-access text providing reproducible empirical examples and hands-on coding exercises in R for cross-sectional and panel data econometrics.

Modern Empirical Strategies: Causal Inference: The Mixtape (Scott Cunningham) - A practitioner-focused, freely accessible online book detailing modern econometric workflows with executable R and Stata implementations for difference-in-differences, matching, and synthetic control methods.

Development Economics & Globalization

This track explores the drivers of global wealth, poverty traps, international trade dynamics, and the institutional mechanisms underlying economic development.

Microeconomic Development & Poverty Traps: 14.771 Development Economics (MIT OpenCourseWare) - A full advanced course with video lectures by Nobel laureate Esther Duflo and Prof. Ben Olken analyzing human capital, credit constraints, land tenure, and governance in low-income nations.

Experimental Evaluation & Field Evidence: Poor Economics: A Radical Rethinking of the Way to Fight Global Poverty (Abhijit V. Banerjee and Esther Duflo) - A landmark empirical study examining evidence from randomized controlled trials on how individuals experiencing poverty navigate education, healthcare, saving, and entrepreneurship.

International Trade Theory & Policy: 14.54 International Trade (MIT OpenCourseWare) - A comprehensive lecture series and problem set collection covering the Ricardian model, Heckscher-Ohlin theorem, gravity equations, and the political economy of tariffs and trade agreements.

Institutions & Long-Run Economic Divergence: Why Nations Fail: The Origins of Power, Prosperity, and Poverty (Daron Acemoglu and James A. Robinson) - The definitive institutional framework demonstrating how historical differences between inclusive and extractive political and economic institutions explain global divergence in prosperity [4.4].

Behavioral & Experimental Economics

This track incorporates psychological insights and experimental methodologies into economic analysis to explain systematic departures from neoclassical rationality.

Psychology and Economic Modeling: 14.13 Psychology and Economics (MIT OpenCourseWare) - A complete university course featuring video lectures by Prof. Frank Schilbach covering present-biased preferences, reference dependence, social preferences, and bounded rationality.

Advanced Behavioral Economic Theory: The Foundations of Behavioral Economic Analysis (Sanjit Dhami) - The encyclopedic treatise offering rigorous mathematical formalizations of prospect theory, hyperbolic discounting, and behavioral game theory alongside empirical test results.

Behavioral Finance & Market Anomalies: Behavioral Finance (Duke University on Coursera) - A free-to-audit course exploring cognitive heuristics, investor overconfidence, loss aversion, and asset pricing anomalies that challenge the efficient market hypothesis.

Choice Architecture & Behavioral Public Policy: Nudge: Improving Decisions About Health, Wealth, and Happiness (Richard H. Thaler and Cass R. Sunstein) - The foundational text for applying behavioral insights to institutional design, default rules, and public policy interventions without restricting choice [4.4].
