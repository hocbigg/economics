# Advanced Topics in Economics

This page branches out from the Economics Core Curriculum into five distinct specialization tracks. Each track represents a genuinely differentiated area of advanced study, with topics that assume prior completion of the core (principles of micro and macro, statistics, history of economic thought, and game theory).

- **Microeconomic Theory & Industrial Organization** — Advanced analysis of individual decision-making, market structure, strategic firm behavior, and the role of government in markets.
- **Macroeconomics & Growth Theory** — Deep dive into aggregate economic analysis, dynamic optimization, long-run growth, and open-economy macroeconomics.
- **Econometrics & Causal Inference** — The empirical toolkit of modern economics: regression analysis, instrumental variables, and research design for answering causal questions.
- **Development & International Economics** — Applied study of poverty, economic growth in developing countries, international trade, and the role of institutions.
- **Behavioral Economics** — The intersection of psychology and economics, covering decision-making under uncertainty, heuristics and biases, and their applications to policy and finance.

## Microeconomic Theory & Industrial Organization

This track deepens the microeconomic analysis from the core, moving from principles-level models to the rigorous theory of consumer and firm behavior, strategic interaction in imperfectly competitive markets, and the economic analysis of government intervention.

Intermediate Microeconomic Theory: [14.04 Intermediate Microeconomic Theory (MIT OpenCourseWare)](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/14-04-intermediate-microeconomic-theory-fall-2020) - a complete MIT course with video lectures by Prof. Robert Townsend, covering consumer choice, producer theory, general equilibrium, contract theory, and mechanism design; the natural next step after principles micro.

Industrial Organization: [14.271 Industrial Organization I (MIT OpenCourseWare)](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/14-271-industrial-organization-i-fall-2022) - a PhD-level introduction to the study of firm behavior and market structure, integrating theoretical models with empirical methods; demanding but rewarding for self-directed learners comfortable with the core.

Public Finance & Public Policy: [14.41 Public Finance and Public Policy (MIT OpenCourseWare)](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/14-41-public-finance-and-public-policy-fall-2024) - a full course with video lectures by Prof. Jonathan Gruber covering taxation, social insurance, public goods, externalities, and government policy toward health care, education, and climate change.

## Macroeconomics & Growth Theory

This track advances the macroeconomic analysis from the core into intermediate theory, dynamic optimization methods, and modern growth theory, providing the analytical foundation for understanding long-run economic development and short-run fluctuations.

Intermediate Macroeconomics: [14.05 Intermediate Macroeconomics (MIT OpenCourseWare)](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/14-05-intermediate-macroeconomics-spring-2013) - a rigorous treatment of consumption, investment, monetary policy, and business cycles at the intermediate level, bridging principles macro and graduate-level material.

Dynamic Optimization Methods: [14.451 Dynamic Optimization Methods with Applications (MIT OpenCourseWare)](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/14-451-dynamic-optimization-methods-with-applications-fall-2009) - covers dynamic programming and optimal control theory, the mathematical toolkit underlying all modern macroeconomic models of growth and fluctuations.

Economic Growth: [Introduction to Modern Economic Growth (Daron Acemoglu)](http://home.cerge-ei.cz/dragana/acemoglu-2007.pdf) - the definitive graduate-level textbook on growth theory, from the neoclassical model to endogenous growth and political economy; a free PDF of the full book is hosted by CERGE-EI.

Open Economy Macroeconomics: [15.015 Macro and International Economics (MIT OpenCourseWare)](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/15-015-macro-and-international-economics-fall-2011) - covers monetary and fiscal policy in open economies, exchange rates, financial crises, and the macroeconomics of development and transition.

## Econometrics & Causal Inference

This track develops the empirical methods that economists use to test theories and answer causal questions with data, covering regression analysis, instrumental variables, panel data methods, and the design of quasi-experimental studies.

Econometric Methods: [14.32 Econometrics (MIT OpenCourseWare)](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/14-32-econometrics-spring-2007) - taught by Prof. Joshua Angrist, covering regression, instrumental variables, simultaneous equations, and program evaluation with problem sets and lecture notes.

Causal Inference & Research Design: [The Effect: An Introduction to Research Design and Causality (Nick Huntington-Klein)](https://theeffectbook.net) - a free, comprehensive online textbook on causal inference with observational data, covering identification, regression, matching, difference-in-differences, and instrumental variables with exceptional clarity and modern examples.

Applied Econometrics in Practice: [Mostly Harmless Econometrics: An Empiricist's Companion (Joshua D. Angrist and Jörn-Steffen Pischke)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780691120355) - the influential guide to the core methods of applied microeconometrics, focused on how researchers actually use regression, instrumental variables, and difference-in-differences to answer causal questions.

## Development & International Economics

This track applies economic theory and empirics to the problems of poverty, economic growth in developing countries, and international trade and finance, combining theoretical models with evidence from randomized trials and natural experiments.

Development Economics: [Development Economics (Marginal Revolution University)](https://learn.mru.org) - a free, video-based course by Tyler Cowen and Alex Tabarrok covering the sources of economic growth, including geography, education, finance, institutions, and culture, with a focus on applied policy questions.

Poverty & Randomized Evaluations: [Poor Economics: A Radical Rethinking of the Way to Fight Global Poverty (Abhijit V. Banerjee and Esther Duflo)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9781610391603) - the influential synthesis of evidence from randomized controlled trials on what works and what does not in fighting global poverty, written by two future Nobel laureates.

International Trade: [14.54 International Trade (MIT OpenCourseWare)](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/14-54-international-trade-fall-2016) - a complete course analyzing why nations trade, who gains from trade, the effects of trade policy, multinationals, and current trade disputes; assumes intermediate microeconomics.

Institutions & Long-run Development: [Why Nations Fail: The Origins of Power, Prosperity, and Poverty (Daron Acemoglu and James A. Robinson)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780307719225) - the accessible synthesis of the institutional approach to economic development, arguing that inclusive political and economic institutions are the fundamental determinant of long-run prosperity.

## Behavioral Economics

This track explores how psychological realism changes standard economic models, covering the foundations of judgment and decision-making, choice architecture, and applications to finance and public policy.

Foundations of Behavioral Economics: [Thinking, Fast and Slow (Daniel Kahneman)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780374533557) - the comprehensive synthesis of the dual-process framework by the Nobel laureate who founded the field, covering heuristics, biases, prospect theory, and overconfidence; the single most important book in behavioral economics.

Choice Architecture & Policy: [Nudge: Improving Decisions About Health, Wealth, and Happiness (Richard H. Thaler and Cass R. Sunstein)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780143115267) - the accessible guide to how "choice architecture" can improve decisions without restricting freedom, written by the Nobel laureate who developed the concept; the practical complement to Kahneman's theoretical foundations.

Behavioral Finance: [Behavioral Finance (Duke University on Coursera)](https://www.coursera.org/learn/duke-behavioral-finance) - a free course covering how psychological biases affect financial decisions, including the disposition effect, framing, and overconfidence in investment contexts.
