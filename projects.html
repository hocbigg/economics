Build your own economic data pipelines

  • Exploring International Debt Statistics Using the World Bank API in Python and R (World Bank) - A step-by-step code walkthrough demonstrating how to programmatically query the World Bank API, clean indicator tables, and export cross-country debt statistics.
  • Economic Data Analysis Project with Python Pandas (Rob Mulla) - A complete video tutorial detailing how to fetch macroeconomic series using the FRED API, clean and merge time-series data, and structure indicators for analysis.
  • fredapi: Python API for FRED (Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis) - A production-ready Python client with documentation and examples for pulling point-in-time vintage data from ALFRED and assembling tidy pandas dataframes.
  • DIME Analytics R Training (World Bank) - A hands-on training repository from the World Bank Development Impact Evaluation group covering reproducible data cleaning, wrangling, and pipeline construction in R.

Build your own interactive economic dashboards

  • Mastering Shiny (Hadley Wickham) - The definitive web guide to developing reactive, interactive data dashboards and web applications using R.
  • Get Started with Streamlit (Streamlit Docs) - Official tutorial on building and deploying data-driven web applications and filterable analytical dashboards in Python.
  • Economic Data Analysis Project with Python Pandas (Rob Mulla) - Video walkthrough building an interactive macroeconomic indicator dashboard with Plotly Express and pandas.

Conduct your own replication studies

  • Guide for Accelerating Computational Reproducibility in the Social Sciences (BITSS / ACRe) - A comprehensive handbook developed with the American Economic Association Data Editor outlining step-by-step protocols for assessing, reproducing, and conducting robustness checks on published economics papers.
  • Development Research in Practice: The DIME Analytics Data Handbook (World Bank) - An open-source operational handbook detailing best practices for code structure, data provenance, and reproducible research workflows in empirical economics.
  • A Guide for Student-led Undergraduate Research in Empirical Microeconomics (Journal of Economics Teaching) - A practical methodological guide walking through the "replicate and extend" model, where learners reproduce a published empirical paper and test novel variations.

Write your own empirical research papers

  • Writing Economics: A Guide for Harvard Economics Concentrators (Harvard Writing Project) - A foundational guide explaining how economists structure empirical papers, formulate testable research hypotheses, document data limitations, and present regression findings.
  • Writing Tips for Economics Research Papers (Plamen Nikolov / IZA) - A detailed technical guide covering empirical paper architecture, model specification descriptions, table layouts, and identification arguments.
  • A Guide for Student-led Undergraduate Research in Empirical Microeconomics (Journal of Economics Teaching) - Follows the lifecycle of an independent empirical paper from identifying data sources to drafting methodology and addressing referee feedback.

Write your own policy briefs

  • Policy Briefs (UNC Writing Center) - A foundational guide to the policy brief genre, detailing audience analysis, executive summary framing, and actionable policy recommendation structures.
  • How to Write a Policy Brief (IDRC - International Development Research Centre) - Practical instructions on translating complex empirical evidence into high-impact, two-page policy memos for non-specialist decision-makers.
  • Policy Briefs 101: A Guide for Translating Research Findings into Policy Impact (California Policy Lab) - A practitioner manual developed for researchers with outlines, graphic design considerations, and editing timelines for policy translations.

Conduct your own cost-benefit analyses

  • OMB Circular No. A-94: Guidelines and Discount Rates for Benefit-Cost Analysis of Federal Programs (The White House) - The official federal standard establishing analytical procedures, net present value calculations, discount rates, and shadow pricing for evaluating public programs.
  • The Green Book: Central Government Guidance on Appraisal and Evaluation (HM Treasury) - The UK government’s benchmark handbook on social cost-benefit analysis, option generation, distributional impact assessment, and risk adjustment.
  • Cost-Benefit Analysis Guidance Note (Australian Government Office of Impact Analysis) - A practical, step-by-step regulatory appraisal guide providing standard valuation techniques, sensitivity tests, and decision criteria.
  • Regional Input-Output Modeling System (RIMS II) User's Guide (U.S. Bureau of Economic Analysis) - Technical guidance detailing how regional input-output multipliers are applied to evaluate the indirect economic effects of investments and policy interventions.
